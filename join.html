The Journey · Theme: Ma

December 10 – 21
12 Days · From Marrakech to the Sahara

Day 1 · Dec 10
Arrival in Marrakech

The medina, the culture, the noise. A first breath before the silence ahead.

Airport pickup, residence in a traditional riad in the UNESCO World Heritage Medina of Marrakech. First contact with Morocco.

Days 2–8 · Dec 11–17
Into the Desert

The desert strips what is unnecessary. Space opens.

Journey south through the Atlas Mountains into the Sahara. Nomadic trekking, bivouac, time at the Dune Hurlante, embodied practices, artistic exploration, co-creation, and silence.

Days 9–10 · Dec 18–19
Artistic Emergence & Integration

Not a performance to produce. An encounter with what has been waiting.

Returning to the fixed bivouac, we enter a more settled phase of the journey. A space for artistic co-creation, self-initiated performance, reflection, integration, and allowing what has emerged in the desert to find its own form.

Days 11–12 · Dec 20–21
Return to Marrakech

Journey back north to Marrakech. One final night in the medina. Departure on Dec 21.

Living nomadically for days, the body attunes to another tempo. Perception sharpens. Time loosens.

Racines du Désert

Walking with the nomads.

Our hosts are nomads from the Draa Valley, born in the desert. Faysal, Abdallah, Ali, Mohamed, Bachir and their team walk, cook and live with you every step of the way.

They share their way of life: the fire made only from dead wood, the water never wasted, the traces left behind always erased. Tea poured slowly, songs and stories under the stars.

This is not a tourist service. It is an invitation into a living culture.

"I grew up in an environment that unites the simplicity of nomads and their noble culture. Today, even settled, at the bottom of my heart I remain a Nomad."Faysal · Desert guide, M'Hamid el Ghizlane

A culture of midwifery

The midwives who hold the space.

This residency is not organized around a central authority, teacher, or guide. Instead, we practice mutual midwifery.

As organizers, we hold the structure and the safe space that allow the residency to unfold, so that each of you can arrive, feel held, and let go. The desert itself becomes the primary guide.

And within that space, each participant carries their own agency, responsibility, and permission in the creative process. No one over-determines the outcome. Together we create the conditions in which something can emerge.

Yi Chen@yi.ankoku

Born in China, Yi Chen is a Butoh-inspired dancer, performance artist, facilitator, and curator. Her work explores a dimensional space bridging the visible and invisible. Drawing from Qigong, Kalaripayattu, Noguchi Taiso, Ankoku Butoh, and the exploration of subconscious and subtle-body states, her practice investigates the body as a site of transformation, perception, and encounter.

- Yi has co-directed and curated numerous international artistic gatherings, including the Space Between Butoh Festival in Bern, Switzerland, and several long-form Butoh residencies and performance laboratories in China. -

She is also a co-creator of the Desert Mirage Journey since its first edition. Having crossed this desert before, she knows that each journey reveals a different landscape, both outwardly and within.

Anys@anys.mechkar

Painter, dancer, and guide of desert retreats and initiatory journeys. His work, on paper and through the body, draws from Butoh, Tao, calligraphy, and elemental movement. Each performance is conceived as a ritual: a psychomagical act that bridges the personal and the universal.

Franco-Moroccan, nomadic by nature. The Sahara is not a destination for him. It is a place of return.

Before you come

Questions

Do I need to be an experienced artist?

No. This residency welcomes artists from any discipline and any level. What matters is the willingness to create, to listen, and to co-create with others. We hold a short call with each applicant to make sure the journey is right for you.

What level of physical fitness is required?

The trek involves walking around 3 to 4 hours per day across sand, at a calm pace, with camels carrying the luggage. No specific training is needed, but you should be comfortable walking and spending full days outdoors. If you have any health concerns, tell us during the call.

What is the weather like in December?

December in the Sahara brings mild, sunny days (around 20°C) and cold nights that can drop near 0°C. Warm layers, a good sleeping bag, and a hat are essential. A full packing list is sent once you are registered.

What language is the residency in?

This is an international gathering, and the residency is conducted entirely in English. Participants come from many countries. It is important that you feel comfortable expressing yourself and understanding others in English, as this is how we will share, create, and hold the space together.

How does payment work?

A 50% transfer confirms your registration, with the balance due before departure. Early bird prices apply until October 10th. A scholarship is available on request. Payment details are shared after your call.
